How to Write Service Pages That Actually Rank (And Sell)

August 22, 2026 · 6 min read

Most service business websites make the same mistake: one page that says "we do everything" and hopes for the best. Google doesn't rank generalists. It ranks specialists.

If you want to get found for "shower door installation in Virginia Beach," you need a page dedicated to that specific service, in that specific city, with specific content that proves you know what you're talking about. A single "Services" page with a bullet list won't cut it.

The Anatomy of a Ranking Service Page

After building and optimizing dozens of service pages for local businesses, here's the exact structure that consistently performs:

1. Unique H1 with Service + City

Your page title must include both the service and the location. "Custom Shower Doors in Virginia Beach" beats "Shower Doors" every time. Google uses the H1 as a strong relevance signal. Make it specific.

2. Scope of Work

Tell the visitor exactly what's included. A bullet list works best: measurement, fabrication, installation, cleanup, warranty. Clarity builds trust. Vagueness kills it.

3. Pricing Transparency

Even if you can't give exact prices online (every job is different), state your pricing philosophy. "Free estimates. Flat-rate pricing. No surprises." beats "Call for quote" every time.

4. Localized FAQ

Answer the questions your customers actually ask. "How long does a shower door installation take in Virginia Beach?" "Do you serve Norfolk?" Each Q&A is a chance to rank for a long-tail search.

5. Internal Links

Link to related services and city pages. A visitor reading about shower doors might also be interested in mirrors or glass partitions. Keep them on your site.

What NOT to Do

  • Don't use the same content on every service page with just the service name swapped — Google penalizes thin content
  • Don't bury your page under too many clicks — every service page should be reachable from the homepage in 2 clicks
  • Don't forget schema markup — Service schema tells Google exactly what you offer
  • Don't skip the photos — a service page without photos is a missed opportunity

The businesses that win locally aren't the ones with the biggest ad budgets. They're the ones with the best-structured websites. Every service page is an opportunity to get found. Make each one count.
